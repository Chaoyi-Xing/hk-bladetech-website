Technical answers directly from our engineering team.

Why choose HK Blade Tech over local suppliers?

Factory Direct + Engineering Expertise. We eliminate middleman markups to deliver uncompromised quality at direct factory prices.

  • Material Options: D2, HSS, carbide and powder-metallurgy grades can be selected according to abrasion, impact load and target service life.
  • Precision Manufacturing: CNC grinding and dimensional inspection help control parallelism, hole position and cutting-edge geometry.
  • Controlled Heat Treatment: Hardness targets are selected to balance wear resistance and toughness for the actual application.
  • Application Support: Material, heat-treatment and coating options are reviewed against the material being cut and operating conditions.

  • Standard Blades: Tell us your machine model (e.g., Cumberland 1215).
  • Custom Knives: Send a photo, drawing, or hand sketch to info@hk-bladetech.com.
How can I extend the life of my machine knives?

We recommend two primary strategies to maximize longevity:

  1. Correct Gap Setting: Follow the machine manufacturer’s recommended rotor-stator clearance. The correct gap depends on the machine, blade geometry and feed material.
  2. Material Selection: Abrasive materials such as glass-filled plastics may require powder-metallurgy steel, coatings or carbide-inlaid blades to extend service life.
How do I know when to replace my blades?

Timely replacement protects your equipment. Watch for these warning signs:

  • Increased Burrs & Dust: Dull blades shatter plastic instead of cutting it cleanly.
  • Abnormal Noise & Vibration: Indicates micro-chipping. Continuing risks severe spindle damage.
  • Higher Amp Draw: Your motor is working significantly harder.
  • Visible Discoloration: Blue-purple oxidation indicates loss of physical properties due to high-friction heat.

Pro Tip: We highly recommend keeping a spare set of blades on the shelf to minimize costly machine downtime during changeovers.

Which blade material is right for my application?

Choosing the correct alloy lowers your cost-per-ton. Here is our quick guide:

  • Standard Plastics (PE/PP/PET): Use Premium D2 / SKD11 for excellent cost-performance.
  • Glass-Filled Plastics: Consider powder-metallurgy steel, a wear-resistant coating or carbide depending on glass content and impact load.
  • Highly Abrasive Materials: Carbide-inlaid blades may reduce changeovers and cost per ton when impact loading is controlled.
  • Film & Foil Slitting: HSS and suitable low-friction coatings can improve edge stability and reduce material adhesion.
